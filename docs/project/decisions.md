- **2026-10-26: Sky discovery pipeline: Strategist, multi-sector searches, Variable Census, Follow-up.** Why nothing new turned up: the analysis searched the most-mined stars (SPOC 2-min targets, which TESS's own pipeline already searched), one sector each, needing ≥ 2 transits, and discarded every eclipsing binary and variable even when no catalog lists them. Now (`web/src/lib/sky/`): **Strategist** (`strategy.ts`) scores every star with a light curve by discovery potential (known planet/TOI host +4, ≥3 sectors +2…4, FFI-only +1.5, brightness, catalogued variable −1) and assigns a playbook: multi-planet (3 sectors, known transits masked), long-period (4 sectors), deep-search (2 sectors, FFI-only), census (1 sector). With Claude (`MODEL`, effort low, ~8 s) it picks 16 of the top 40 and writes the strategy; unknown TICs are dropped and the plan filled from the heuristic. **Light Curves** stitch the densest run of sectors (span ≤ 5 sector numbers, `sectorsFor`) and hold the rest out. **Transit Hunter**: prewhitens pulsations faster than 1.5 d (refined Lomb-Scargle peaks, up to 5), masks known planets' transit *and* phase-0.5 windows (archive `pl_tranmid`/`pl_trandur`), then iterative BLS (cut out what passes, search again; peaks at a cut-out period are residue, not reported). BLS now uses a geometric frequency grid (relative step = 0.5 × shortest duration / baseline): same old calibration tests pass, 1 sector 0.8 → 0.23 s, 4 sectors 10.7 → 2.5 s. **Single-Transit Hunter** (`singles.ts`): box dips ≥ 8σ of the same-length box-average scatter (red-noise aware), away from gap edges, flat on both sides; dips within 0.5 d on another star are systematics. **Vetter** adds AAVSO VSX contamination, a maximum-transit-duration check from the header's radius/logg, pulsation harmonics (3–6× a fast LS period), and a new verdict "Uncatalogued eclipsing binary" (binary signs from its own light curve, no VSX/SIMBAD variable entry, VSX lookup ok). **Variable Census** (`vsx.ts`, VizieR `B/vsx/vsx`, ~1.3 s): variables not in VSX/SIMBAD and with period ≤ baseline/3 are "Uncatalogued variable"; same-period on ≥ 2 stars is systematic. **Follow-up**: Claude reviews the finds (lab-notebook notes citing ids) and orders ≤ 5 actions: `recheck` (BLS at ±1% of the period on a held-out sector) or `extend` (up to 3 more sectors, fresh search: a single dip can turn periodic); heuristic fallback. New events `analysis-note`, plan on `analysis-start`; the UI's "Discovery agents" panel shows notebook, new finds (gold, with follow-up result), rejected signals and per-star playbook. - **2026-10-26: Deep sky surveys: uncapped background jobs.** The quick discovery run stops at 150 s, 16 stars and 4 sectors. A **deep survey** ("Deep survey" button on `/sky`) instead searches every star in the cone that has a TESS light curve, every sector, as a background job that keeps going with the tab closed. **Plan:** a full inventory (up to 6 pages of SPOC, plus SIMBAD stars to V 13.5 (≤2000) checked for QLP/TESS-SPOC HLSP files 80 TICs at a time), one cross-match of the whole field (planets, TOIs, EB neighbours, VSX), and each star's sectors split into seasons of ≤4 nearby sectors (`seasonsFor`, best pipeline per sector, latest first). The Strategist (Claude or heuristic) orders the first 20 stars; the rest follow by discovery score. **Run:** `runSurveyStep` takes a lease on the survey row, searches seasons 4 at a time for ~230 s (`SKY_SURVEY_STEP_MS`), saves every 4 s, then chains to the next step with an HMAC-keyed POST to `/api/sky/survey/:id/step` that runs in `after()`. A GET of a survey whose lease expired with no update for 90 s restarts the chain, so a crashed step costs at most one step. Stop wins over a running step's save. A star with a signal or dip gets its other seasons searched next. A failed download gets one retry. At 25/50/75/100 % Claude reviews the finds so far (`claudeSurveyReview`: notebook notes citing ids, plus up to 5 TICs to prioritise). **Finds across seasons** (`surveyObjects`): periodic signals are clustered across seasons ("seen in N of M seasons", or "not seen" where a season covered ≥2 transits); lone dips on one star >2 d apart with similar depth and duration become **duotransits** with the periods the coverage allows. A third dip that fits pins the period; one that doesn't adds a caution. Dips on several stars at once are systematic. **Storage:** SpacetimeDB tables `sky_surveys` + `sky_survey_stars` (lease checked in the reducers) when it's configured, else JSON files (`SKY_SURVEY_DIR`, for dev). At most `SKY_SURVEY_MAX_RUNNING` (3) at once. Shareable link `/sky?survey=<id>`. - **2026-10-27: Follow-up vetting of a find (staged, tiered).** Survey finds were labelled by one pass of quick checks, and a check that didn't run read the same as one that passed. Now each transit find has a **vet** button (`POST /api/sky/vet`, `web/src/lib/sky/vetReport.ts`, budget 200 s) that runs a staged vetting, cheapest and most decisive first. Stage 0 checks the host star and catalogs (TIC, Gaia DR3 RUWE/NSS, ExoFOP, CTOIs, TESS EB catalog, VSX, planets/TOIs/KOIs) and is a hard gate: known means tier K, star-sized or a catalogued binary means X, both before any download. Stages 1-3 run on every sector of the star (≤ 16): a per-transit table with timing and a linear ephemeris fit, SNR growth, period and alias tests, four detrendings with transits masked, depth stability, odd/even, secondary at any phase, trapezoid shape, duration and density against the star, variability at the period, centroid shift, neighbours bright enough to cause the dip, saturation, crowding, cross-pipeline depth, another year. Stage 4 recommends follow-up (next transit times, RV amplitudes), and the tier (A/B/C/X/K) is Stage 5. Every check is logged as pass / flag / fail / not run, and difference imaging (needs TPFs) is always "not run", so in practice nothing reaches A. Not scored: QLP small/large aperture depths, because on WASP-18 b (on target, isolated) they differ 3x, so the comparison can't detect a blend. Also fixed: duotransits weren't counted as new finds (`isNewFind`), so the survey hid its best candidate among the rejected ones. Checked live: WASP-18 b → K in 1.4 s (forced through: 215 transits, P to 4e-8 d, a 0.40 ppt occultation passed as thermal emission, density 0.67× catalog); TIC 40976268 duotransit → C (subgiant host, 2 transits, aliases 676.4/338.2 d, 18 TIC stars within 1′ could make the dip; both aliases' next transit is 2026-11-10 05:38 UTC); TIC 40976339 single → C (P > 69.7 d from coverage, P ≳ 24 d from duration).

- **2026-10-27: SIMBAD made resilient.** Every space run reported "SIMBAD (CDS) failed: timed out after 17s". From the dev sandbox CDS doesn't answer at all, and one run sends 3 SIMBAD queries at once (the SIMBAD source, the field-star list, EB neighbours), against CDS's ~5-6 queries/s blacklist. All SIMBAD calls now go through `simbadTap` (`web/src/lib/sky/simbad.ts`): POST, per-host throttle (300 ms spacing, ≤ 3 in flight), a hedged race with the official CfA mirror (`simbad.cfa.harvard.edu`, started after 3 s or as soon as CDS fails), and one overall deadline. If both fail, the SIMBAD source shows the cone's brightest Gaia DR3 stars from ESA, with a note (no galaxies, nebulae or common names). Checked live with CDS unreachable: Orion 0.5° gives 20 Gaia stars in 9.9 s instead of an error. Follow-up the same day: from the user's network both CDS and the CfA mirror give no answer at all (curl `000` after 20-30 s, IPv4 too, DNS fine) while ESA answers in ~1 s, so it's the network, not bot blocking. `simbadTap` now remembers silence from both hosts for 5 minutes and fails at once, so the next runs go straight to Gaia instead of waiting ~8 s each time.

- **2026-10-27: Survey signals measured at their period in other seasons.** Survey finds were often "not seen in other seasons" because every season is searched blind: a weak signal (SNR ~8 in its best season) falls below the detection bar (SDE 9, SNR 7.1) where there are fewer transits or more noise, or loses the top BLS peak to pulsation or scattered light. Now each signal is also measured at its period in the star's other seasons (`forcedCheck`, `web/src/lib/sky/analysis.ts`): BLS held to its duration and ±0.5% of its period with free phase (one season's ephemeris drifts by more than a transit over a year), red-noise errors, and a noise floor from 13 null searches (inverted flux, periods ±3-10% off), since a free-phase search always finds some dip: with only 4 nulls, pure noise came out "weak" 25% of the time. Results: recovered / weak / absent / inconclusive, and the label says which ("absent" is the real warning). Seasons searched before the signal appeared are queued for a re-download and check (`nextSeason` returns `recheck`). Calibrated on 20 noise seeds per case: a 1.2 ppt dip in 1.5 ppt/2-min noise is recovered 20/20; a 0.3 ppt dip is weak 18/20 (2 inconclusive); no dip with 0.3 ppt expected is weak 1/20; no dip with 1.2 ppt expected is absent 20/20.
